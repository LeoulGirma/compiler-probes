What did .NET 11 actually change?

The answer

Of six .NET 11 claims, two delivered large wins (devirtualisation, 6.5× and 6.3× in our probes), runtime async is real but opt-in (awaits about 4× cheaper), Math.BigMul emits fewer instructions yet runs 31% slower, and two showed no change.

The question

Do the runtime changes listed for .NET 11 show up when measured?

Checked on a second machine

The .NET 10 and .NET 11 JIT columns were rerun on a different CPU and OS: a 4-vCPU cloud server (Intel Haswell, Ubuntu Linux), five rounds with alternating order and its other workloads paused. .NET 11 against .NET 10, median of rounds.

Every result holds. The devirtualisation wins are larger on the older server, runtime async cuts the cost of an await on both, and Math.BigMul is slower on both: the stack round-trip in the loop costs more on Haswell, not less. Absolute times are not comparable between the two machines and are not shown.
